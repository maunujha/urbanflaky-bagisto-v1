A good t-shirt is not finished when it leaves the shop. Heavyweight cotton is built to soften, settle and take on the shape of the person wearing it — but only if it is looked after. Most of the damage that ends a tee early happens in the wash, the dryer and the wardrobe, not on the body. The good news: the habits that protect cotton are simple, cheap and take no extra time once they become routine.

This guide covers the essentials for cotton t-shirts in general and for heavyweight oversized tees in particular, where a dense knit and a deliberate silhouette are worth protecting.

Know what you are washing

Cotton is a natural fibre. It absorbs water readily, swells as it does, and tightens as it dries — which is why heat is its biggest enemy. Heavyweight jersey, generally anything that feels dense and holds its shape off the hanger, is more forgiving than thin fabric, but it still responds to the same three variables: water temperature, agitation and drying heat.

Before the first wash, read the care label. It is the manufacturer's own instruction for that specific fabric and finish, and it outranks any general advice — including this article. Our full garment care guide covers label symbols in detail.

Wash cold, wash inside out

Cold or cool water (30°C or below) is the single most effective change you can make. It prevents shrinkage, keeps black from fading to charcoal, and protects printed graphics from cracking.

  • Turn every tee inside out. The outer face — the side people see — avoids rubbing against the drum and other garments, so it pills and fades far more slowly.
  • Wash with similar colours and weights. Black with black, light with light. Avoid washing tees alongside denim, zips and hooks, which abrade the surface.
  • Do not overload the machine. Clothes need room to move through the water; a packed drum creates friction and leaves detergent behind.
  • Choose a gentle or delicate cycle with a lower spin speed where possible.

Use less detergent than you think

Excess detergent does not clean better. It lingers in the fibres, stiffens the fabric and dulls dark colours over time. Use a mild liquid detergent at the dose recommended for a light load, and skip bleach entirely on coloured garments. For black tees, a detergent formulated for darks helps hold the depth of the colour wash after wash.

Fabric softener is optional. It can make cotton feel smoother at first, but it coats the fibres and reduces their natural breathability. Heavyweight cotton softens on its own with wear.

Skip the dryer

Tumble drying is where most shrinkage happens. High heat contracts cotton fibres and, on an oversized tee, can pull the length and the drop of the shoulder out of proportion.

  • Air-dry flat or on a wide hanger, away from harsh direct sunlight, which bleaches dark fabric.
  • Reshape while damp. Gently straighten the hem, the collar and the side seams by hand before the tee dries.
  • If you must use a dryer, choose the lowest heat setting and remove the garment while it is still slightly damp.

Protect the collar

A stretched, wavy neckline is the first sign of a tired tee. Avoid pulling shirts on and off by the collar, and never hang a wet t-shirt from a narrow hanger — the weight of the water drags the neck and shoulders out of shape. A ribbed, well-constructed collar recovers well, but repeated strain wins eventually.

Ironing and steaming

Heavyweight cotton rarely needs much pressing; hanging it in a steamy bathroom often does the job. If you iron, turn the tee inside out and use a medium setting. Never iron directly over a print — press around it or place a cloth between the iron and the graphic.

Store tees folded, not hung

Gravity is slow but persistent. Hanging a heavy t-shirt for months stretches the shoulders and lengthens the body. Fold tees and stack them with the heaviest at the bottom; it keeps their shape and makes a monochrome wardrobe easy to read at a glance.

Caring for black

Black is the colour that shows wear first. To keep it deep:

  • Wash less often — air a tee between wears if it is not actually dirty.
  • Always wash inside out, in cold water, with other darks.
  • Dry in the shade.
  • Keep deodorant to dry skin before dressing to avoid white marks that need extra washing to remove.

Why care matters more for oversized fits

An oversized tee depends on proportion: a dropped shoulder, a wider body and a sleeve that falls at the right point on the arm. Shrinkage of even a few centimetres changes the silhouette entirely, turning a relaxed fit into one that simply looks a size too small. Washing cold and air-drying preserves the cut you chose. A simple routine

  1. Turn inside out.
  2. Wash cold with similar colours, on a gentle cycle.
  3. Use a mild detergent, no bleach.
  4. Reshape and air-dry in the shade.
  5. Fold and store.
